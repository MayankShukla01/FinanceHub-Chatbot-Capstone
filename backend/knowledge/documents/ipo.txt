# IPO (Initial Public Offering) in India — Complete Guide

## What is an IPO?

An IPO (Initial Public Offering) is the process through which a private company offers its shares to the public for the first time on a stock exchange. After an IPO, the company becomes a "publicly listed" company, and its shares can be bought and sold by anyone on the stock exchange (BSE or NSE).

**Why do companies go public?**
1. **Raise capital**: To fund expansion, pay off debt, or invest in new projects
2. **Liquidity for founders**: Early investors and founders can sell their shares
3. **Brand visibility**: Being listed increases credibility and brand recognition
4. **Employee benefits**: Can offer ESOPs (Employee Stock Ownership Plans) with tradeable shares
5. **Acquisition currency**: Listed shares can be used for mergers and acquisitions

## The IPO Process in India

### 1. Preparation Phase
- Company appoints investment banks (called Book Running Lead Managers or BRLMs) to manage the IPO
- Prepares a Draft Red Herring Prospectus (DRHP) — a detailed document about the company's business, financials, risks, and purpose of the IPO
- DRHP is filed with SEBI for approval
- SEBI reviews and approves the prospectus

### 2. Price Discovery — Book Building Process
Most IPOs in India use the **Book Building** method:
- Company sets a **price band** (e.g., ₹500 - ₹550 per share)
- Investors bid within this range
- Based on demand at various price levels, the final **issue price** (cut-off price) is determined
- If demand is high, the issue price is set at the upper end of the band

**Fixed Price IPO** (less common): The company sets a fixed price, and investors apply at that price.

### 3. IPO Opens for Subscription
- IPO is open for bidding for 3-5 working days
- Three categories of investors:
  - **Retail Individual Investors (RII)**: Investment up to ₹2 lakh (35% of shares reserved)
  - **Non-Institutional Investors (NII/HNI)**: Investment above ₹2 lakh (15% of shares reserved)
  - **Qualified Institutional Buyers (QIB)**: Mutual funds, banks, FIIs (50% of shares reserved)

### 4. Allotment
- If demand exceeds supply (oversubscribed), allotment is done by lottery for retail investors
- If subscribed 1x or less, all applicants get shares
- If oversubscribed, each retail applicant has an equal chance of getting at least 1 lot
- Allotment is typically done within 6 days after IPO closes

### 5. Listing
- Shares are listed on BSE/NSE
- The listing price may be higher (listing gains), lower (listing at discount), or at the issue price
- After listing, shares trade freely on the exchange

## Key IPO Terms

### Lot Size
- Minimum number of shares you must apply for
- Set by the company so that minimum application value is between ₹13,000 - ₹15,000
- Example: If share price is ₹500, lot size might be 30 shares (₹15,000 minimum)

### Price Band
- Range within which investors can bid (e.g., ₹780 - ₹800)
- Lower end = floor price, Upper end = cap price
- Retail investors can bid at "Cut-off" price — meaning they're willing to pay whatever the final price is

### ASBA (Application Supported by Blocked Amount)
- When you apply for an IPO, the application money is not debited from your account
- Instead, it is **blocked** (like a hold) in your bank account
- Money is debited only if you get allotment
- If you don't get allotment, the block is removed and money remains in your account
- This means you earn interest on the blocked amount during the IPO process

### UPI Mandate
- Retail investors can apply for IPOs using UPI
- When you apply through a broker app, you receive a UPI mandate request on your UPI app (Google Pay, PhonePe, BHIM)
- You must **approve** this mandate within the deadline (usually before IPO closes)
- **IMPORTANT**: If you don't approve the UPI mandate, your application is rejected

## How to Apply for an IPO

### Through a Broker App (Most Common Method):
1. Open your broker's app (Zerodha, Groww, Angel One, Upstox)
2. Go to the IPO section
3. Select the IPO you want to apply for
4. Choose the number of lots
5. Enter your bid price (or select "Cut-off")
6. Enter your UPI ID (e.g., yourname@upi)
7. Submit the application
8. Open your UPI app (Google Pay, PhonePe, BHIM)
9. Approve the mandate request
10. Done! Wait for allotment results

### Through Net Banking (ASBA):
1. Log in to your bank's net banking
2. Go to "IPO Application" or "ASBA" section
3. Select the IPO
4. Fill in details and submit
5. Amount is blocked in your bank account

## Understanding IPO Subscription

IPO subscription shows how many times the IPO has been applied for compared to shares available.

- **1x subscribed**: Demand equals supply — everyone gets shares
- **3x subscribed**: 3 times more applications than available shares
- **50x subscribed**: 50 times more demand — very high competition

Example of subscription categories:
- Retail (RII): 10.5x subscribed
- HNI (NII): 25x subscribed
- QIB: 45x subscribed
- Overall: 30x subscribed

Higher subscription = lower chance of allotment for retail investors. In highly oversubscribed IPOs, retail allotment is done by computerized lottery.

## Grey Market Premium (GMP)

GMP is the unofficial premium at which IPO shares are trading in the grey (unofficial) market before listing.

- **Not regulated** by SEBI — purely speculative
- Indicates expected listing gains
- Example: If IPO price is ₹500 and GMP is ₹200, the expected listing price is ₹700
- **Warning**: GMP is unreliable and can change rapidly. Don't make investment decisions based solely on GMP.

## Listing Gains

Listing gains refer to the profit made if the stock lists above the IPO price.

- If you got allotment at ₹500 and the stock lists at ₹700, your listing gain is ₹200 per share (40%)
- Some investors apply for IPOs specifically for listing gains and sell on day one
- Not all IPOs give positive listing gains — some list at a discount (below IPO price)

## SME IPOs

SME (Small and Medium Enterprise) IPOs are IPOs by smaller companies listed on BSE SME or NSE Emerge platforms.

**Key differences from mainboard IPOs:**
- Smaller issue size (typically ₹10-50 crore)
- Higher lot sizes (₹1-2 lakh minimum investment)
- Higher risk but potentially higher returns
- Less analyst coverage and liquidity
- Market maker ensures minimum trading activity for 3 years
- Often see very high subscription (100x-500x)

**Caution**: SME IPOs carry significantly higher risk. Many SME stocks fall sharply after initial listing gains. Only invest money you can afford to lose.

## Other Types of Public Offerings

### FPO (Follow-on Public Offering)
- When an already listed company issues additional shares to raise more capital
- Existing shareholders' stake gets diluted

### Rights Issue
- Existing shareholders are given the right to buy additional shares at a discounted price
- Proportional to their current holding
- Example: 1:5 rights issue means for every 5 shares you hold, you can buy 1 new share at the rights price

### OFS (Offer for Sale)
- Existing shareholders (promoters, PE investors) sell their shares to the public
- Company doesn't receive any money — it goes to the selling shareholders
- Common when promoters want to reduce their stake

## Tips for IPO Investing

1. **Read the prospectus**: At minimum, read the "Risk Factors" and "Objects of the Issue" sections
2. **Check company financials**: Look at revenue growth, profit margins, and debt levels
3. **Compare with peers**: Is the company priced fairly compared to listed competitors?
4. **Don't apply blindly**: Not every IPO is worth investing in
5. **Don't borrow to invest**: Never take loans to apply for IPOs
6. **Consider long-term holding**: Some IPOs may not give listing gains but can be good long-term investments
7. **Approve UPI mandate promptly**: Don't forget to approve the mandate on your UPI app
8. **Apply at cut-off**: Retail investors should usually apply at cut-off price for best allotment chances
