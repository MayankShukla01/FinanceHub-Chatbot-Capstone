# Bonds and Savings Instruments in India

## Government Bonds (G-Sec)

Government Securities (G-Sec) are debt instruments issued by the Government of India to raise money. When you buy a G-Sec, you're essentially lending money to the government, and they pay you interest.

**Key features:**
- Issued by the Reserve Bank of India (RBI) on behalf of the government
- Considered the safest investment (sovereign guarantee — government cannot default)
- Fixed interest rate (coupon rate) paid semi-annually
- Available in various tenures: 5, 10, 15, 20, 30, and 40 years
- Can be bought through RBI Retail Direct, stock brokers, or banks
- Minimum investment: ₹10,000

**Types of Government Securities:**
1. **Fixed Rate Bonds**: Pay a fixed coupon rate throughout the tenure
2. **Floating Rate Bonds (FRBs)**: Interest rate changes periodically based on a benchmark
3. **State Development Loans (SDLs)**: Issued by state governments, slightly higher yields

### Treasury Bills (T-Bills)

T-Bills are short-term government securities with maturity periods of 91 days, 182 days, or 364 days.

- Issued at a discount to face value (e.g., buy at ₹97, get ₹100 at maturity)
- No periodic interest payment — your return is the difference between purchase price and face value
- Very safe and liquid
- Minimum investment: ₹25,000
- Auctioned by RBI weekly

## Sovereign Gold Bonds (SGBs)

Sovereign Gold Bonds are government securities denominated in grams of gold. They are an alternative to holding physical gold.

**Key features:**
- Issued by RBI on behalf of the Government of India
- Denominated in grams of gold (minimum 1 gram)
- Maximum investment: 4 kg per individual per financial year
- Tenure: 8 years (with exit option after 5th year)
- Interest: 2.5% per annum on the issue price (paid semi-annually)
- Capital gains tax exemption if held till maturity
- No storage or purity concerns (unlike physical gold)
- Can be traded on stock exchanges

**How to buy SGBs:**
- Through banks, post offices, stock exchanges, and Stock Holding Corporation
- Online purchase gives ₹50 discount per gram
- Issued in tranches by RBI (announced periodically)

**Example**: If you buy 10 grams of SGB at ₹5,000/gram (₹50,000 total), you earn 2.5% interest (₹1,250/year) plus any increase in gold price. If gold rises to ₹6,500/gram at maturity, your investment is worth ₹65,000 + interest earned.

## Public Provident Fund (PPF)

PPF is one of India's most popular long-term savings schemes, backed by the government.

**Key features:**
- Current interest rate: 7.1% per annum (compounded annually, revised quarterly by the government)
- Tenure: 15 years (can be extended in blocks of 5 years)
- Minimum investment: ₹500 per year
- Maximum investment: ₹1,50,000 per year
- Tax benefits under Section 80C (investment is tax-deductible)
- Interest earned is completely tax-free
- Maturity amount is also tax-free (EEE — Exempt, Exempt, Exempt)
- Partial withdrawal allowed after 7 years
- Loan facility available from 3rd to 6th year

**PPF falls under the EEE (Exempt-Exempt-Exempt) tax category:**
- Investment is exempt (Section 80C deduction)
- Interest earned is exempt
- Maturity amount is exempt

**Where to open PPF:**
- Post offices
- Banks (SBI, HDFC, ICICI, etc.)
- Online through net banking

**Best for**: Conservative investors looking for guaranteed, tax-free returns over 15+ years.

## National Pension System (NPS)

NPS is a government-backed retirement savings scheme designed to provide pension after retirement.

**Key features:**
- Open to all Indian citizens aged 18-70 years
- Two types of accounts:
  - **Tier-I**: Main pension account (restricted withdrawal, tax benefits)
  - **Tier-II**: Voluntary savings account (flexible withdrawal, no special tax benefits)
- Investment options: Equity (up to 75%), Corporate Bonds, Government Bonds, Alternative Assets
- Very low fund management charges (0.01% - 0.09%)
- Tax benefits:
  - Section 80CCD(1): Up to ₹1.5 lakh (within 80C limit)
  - Section 80CCD(1B): Additional ₹50,000 deduction (over and above 80C)
  - Total tax benefit possible: ₹2,00,000
- At retirement (age 60): Minimum 40% must be used to buy annuity, 60% can be withdrawn tax-free
- Lock-in until age 60 (with limited withdrawal options for specific purposes)

**Pension Fund Managers**: SBI Pension Fund, LIC Pension Fund, HDFC Pension Fund, Kotak Pension Fund, etc.

**Best for**: Long-term retirement planning with additional tax benefits beyond Section 80C.

## Employees' Provident Fund (EPF)

EPF is a mandatory retirement savings scheme for salaried employees.

**Key features:**
- Both employee and employer contribute 12% of basic salary each month
- Current interest rate: 8.25% per annum (for FY 2023-24)
- Managed by EPFO (Employees' Provident Fund Organisation)
- Employee contribution qualifies for Section 80C deduction
- Interest and maturity are tax-free (if service > 5 years)
- Can be withdrawn fully on retirement, resignation (after 2 months), or for specific purposes (home purchase, medical emergency, education)
- Accessible via UMANG app and EPFO portal

**EPF vs PPF:**
| Feature | EPF | PPF |
|---------|-----|-----|
| Eligibility | Salaried employees | Anyone |
| Interest Rate | 8.25% | 7.1% |
| Employer Contribution | Yes (12%) | No |
| Lock-in | Until retirement | 15 years |
| Tax Benefit | Section 80C | Section 80C |

## Fixed Deposits (FDs)

Fixed Deposits are the most traditional savings instrument in India, offered by banks and NBFCs.

**Key features:**
- Fixed interest rate for a fixed tenure (7 days to 10 years)
- Current FD rates: 6% - 7.5% (varies by bank and tenure)
- Interest can be paid monthly, quarterly, or at maturity (cumulative)
- Premature withdrawal allowed with a penalty (0.5% - 1%)
- Deposit insurance up to ₹5 lakh per bank (DICGC insurance)
- Senior citizens get 0.25% - 0.50% extra interest
- Tax-saver FD (5-year lock-in) qualifies for Section 80C

**Important**: FD interest is fully taxable. TDS (Tax Deducted at Source) is deducted at 10% if interest exceeds ₹40,000/year (₹50,000 for senior citizens).

**Popular banks for FDs**: SBI, HDFC Bank, ICICI Bank, Axis Bank, and small finance banks (which offer higher rates).

## RBI Retail Direct

RBI Retail Direct is a platform launched by the Reserve Bank of India that allows individual investors to buy government securities directly.

**Key features:**
- Free to open and maintain
- Buy G-Sec, T-Bills, SGBs, and SDLs directly from RBI
- No intermediary charges
- Accessible via rbiretaildirect.org.in
- KYC through PAN, Aadhaar, and bank account
- Securities held in a Subsidiary General Ledger (SGL) account with RBI

**How to open:**
1. Visit rbiretaildirect.org.in
2. Register with PAN, Aadhaar, bank account, and Demat details
3. Complete video KYC
4. Start buying government securities in primary auctions or secondary market

## Corporate Bonds

Corporate bonds are debt instruments issued by companies to raise capital.

**Key features:**
- Higher interest rates than government bonds (8% - 12% typical)
- Higher risk than G-Sec (depends on company's creditworthiness)
- Credit rating by agencies like CRISIL, ICRA, CARE (AAA is safest, D is default)
- Available on stock exchanges and through brokers
- Interest is taxable as income

**Risk tip**: Only invest in bonds rated AA or above. Lower-rated bonds offer higher returns but carry significant default risk.
