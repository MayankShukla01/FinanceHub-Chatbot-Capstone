# Portfolio Management for Indian Investors

## What is a Portfolio?

A portfolio is the collection of all your investments. It can include stocks, mutual funds, bonds, fixed deposits, real estate, gold, and any other assets you own. Portfolio management is the art and science of selecting and managing these investments to achieve your financial goals.

## Diversification

Diversification means spreading your investments across different asset classes, sectors, and instruments to reduce risk. The idea is simple: "Don't put all your eggs in one basket."

**Why diversify?**
- If one investment performs poorly, others may perform well, balancing your overall returns
- Reduces the impact of any single investment failing
- Smooths out the ups and downs of your portfolio

**How to diversify:**

### Across Asset Classes
- **Equity (Stocks/Mutual Funds)**: For growth (higher risk, higher return)
- **Debt (Bonds/FDs/PPF)**: For stability (lower risk, predictable return)
- **Gold (SGBs/Gold ETFs)**: Hedge against inflation and market crashes
- **Real Estate**: Long-term appreciation and rental income
- **Cash/Liquid Funds**: For emergencies and opportunities

### Within Equity
- **Across market caps**: Large-cap, mid-cap, and small-cap stocks
- **Across sectors**: IT, banking, pharma, FMCG, auto, energy
- **Across geographies**: Indian stocks + international funds (e.g., Nasdaq 100 fund)

### Common Mistakes in Diversification
- **Over-diversification**: Owning 30-50 stocks doesn't add value — 10-15 well-chosen stocks are enough
- **Diworsification**: Buying many similar funds (e.g., 5 large-cap funds) — they'll all behave similarly
- **No diversification**: Putting all money in one stock or sector

## Asset Allocation

Asset allocation is deciding what percentage of your portfolio goes into each asset class. It's the most important investment decision you'll make — studies show that asset allocation accounts for over 90% of portfolio returns variability.

### Asset Allocation by Age (Rule of Thumb)

A common guideline is: **Equity allocation = 100 - Your Age**

| Age | Equity | Debt | Gold |
|-----|--------|------|------|
| 25 years | 75% | 20% | 5% |
| 35 years | 65% | 25% | 10% |
| 45 years | 55% | 35% | 10% |
| 55 years | 40% | 50% | 10% |
| 65+ years | 25% | 65% | 10% |

This is a general guideline. Your actual allocation should depend on:
- **Risk tolerance**: How much volatility can you stomach?
- **Time horizon**: When do you need the money?
- **Financial goals**: Retirement, child's education, house purchase
- **Income stability**: Stable government job vs. freelancing

### Sample Portfolio for a 28-Year-Old Beginner (₹20,000/month SIP)

| Investment | Amount | Allocation | Purpose |
|-----------|--------|-----------|---------|
| Nifty 50 Index Fund | ₹6,000 | 30% | Core equity — large cap |
| Flexi Cap Fund | ₹5,000 | 25% | Diversified equity |
| Mid Cap Fund | ₹3,000 | 15% | Growth |

## Risk Profiling

Understanding your risk appetite is crucial before investing. There are three broad risk profiles:

### Conservative Investor
- Cannot tolerate losses
- Prefers guaranteed returns
- Suitable investments: FDs, PPF, Debt mutual funds, Government bonds
- Expected returns: 6-8% annually
- Time horizon: Short to medium term (1-5 years)

### Moderate Investor
- Can tolerate some volatility
- Wants a mix of growth and safety
- Suitable investments: Hybrid mutual funds, large-cap funds, balanced portfolio
- Expected returns: 8-12% annually
- Time horizon: Medium to long term (5-10 years)

### Aggressive Investor
- Comfortable with significant short-term losses for long-term gains
- Suitable investments: Small-cap funds, mid-cap stocks, sectoral funds
- Expected returns: 12-18% annually (with high volatility)
- Time horizon: Long term (10+ years)

### How to Assess Your Risk Profile
Ask yourself:
1. If your investment dropped 20% in a month, would you: (a) Sell immediately (b) Hold and wait (c) Buy more?
2. How soon do you need this money?
3. Do you have other income sources or safety nets?
4. How would a loss affect your daily life?

Most investment platforms (Groww, Zerodha) offer risk assessment questionnaires.

## Portfolio Rebalancing

Rebalancing is the process of bringing your portfolio back to its original target allocation.

**Why rebalance?**
Over time, different asset classes grow at different rates, causing your allocation to drift from the target.

**Example:**
- Target allocation: 70% equity, 30% debt
- After a bull market: Portfolio becomes 80% equity, 20% debt (equity grew faster)
- Rebalancing: Sell some equity and buy debt to return to 70:30

**How often to rebalance?**
- **Time-based**: Every 6 months or annually
- **Threshold-based**: When any asset class deviates by more than 5-10% from target
- **Annual rebalancing** is most practical for retail investors

**How to rebalance:**
1. Check current allocation vs target allocation
2. Sell overweight assets (those that have grown beyond target %)
3. Buy underweight assets (those that have fallen below target %)
4. Consider tax implications before selling (STCG/LTCG)

## Emergency Fund

Before investing in the stock market, you MUST build an emergency fund.

**What is an emergency fund?**
- Money set aside for unexpected expenses (medical emergency, job loss, urgent repairs)
- Should cover 6 months of essential expenses

**How much to keep?**
- Calculate monthly essential expenses (rent, food, EMIs, insurance, utilities)
- Multiply by 6
- Example: ₹40,000/month expenses × 6 = ₹2,40,000 emergency fund

**Where to keep it?**
- Savings account (instant access)
- Liquid mutual funds (slightly higher returns, redeemable in 1 day)
- Fixed deposit (with premature withdrawal facility)
- DO NOT invest emergency fund in stocks, mutual funds, or any volatile instrument

## Goal-Based Investing

Instead of investing randomly, assign each investment to a specific financial goal:

| Goal | Timeline | Investment Type | Example |
|------|----------|----------------|---------|
| Emergency fund | Immediate | Savings + Liquid fund | ₹3 lakh in savings |
| Vacation | 1-2 years | Short-term debt fund | ₹2,000/month SIP |
| Car down payment | 3 years | Conservative hybrid fund | ₹5,000/month SIP |
| House down payment | 5-7 years | Flexi cap + Large cap fund | ₹15,000/month SIP |
| Child's education | 15 years | Equity + ELSS | ₹10,000/month SIP |
| Retirement | 25-30 years | Aggressive equity + NPS | ₹20,000/month SIP |

## Common Portfolio Mistakes

1. **No emergency fund**: Investing before securing an emergency fund leads to forced selling during crises
2. **Chasing past performance**: Last year's best fund may be next year's worst
3. **Panic selling during crashes**: Market crashes are temporary — stay invested
4. **No clear goals**: Investing without knowing why leads to poor decisions
5. **Ignoring inflation**: ₹1 crore today won't be worth ₹1 crore in 20 years — plan for inflation
6. **Too much in one stock**: Even great companies can fall — limit any single stock to 5-10% of portfolio
7. **Not reviewing portfolio**: Review at least annually to check if your investments align with goals
8. **Emotional investing**: Don't buy or sell based on news headlines, WhatsApp tips, or fear/greed
9. **Ignoring insurance**: Health and term life insurance are essential before investing
10. **Not starting at all**: The biggest mistake is waiting for the "perfect time" — start now, start small
